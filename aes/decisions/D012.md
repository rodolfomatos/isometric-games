# D012 — the 2026 reimagining, and the art on disk is not it

## The question

Three visual languages were in play and none of them had been chosen:

1. **1987.** Ten screenshots of the Spectrum original and the modern remake, kept
   outside the repository as reference. Decorated walls carrying the identity,
   small isometric props standing inside their tile, a plain grid floor.
2. **2026.** `docs/REIMAGINING.md`, 1278 lines, written before this session:
   *"uma interpretação moderna de Head over Heels que preserve a identidade
   visual, a linguagem isométrica, as proporções, os elementos reconhecíveis e o
   espírito do original"* — with the Spectrum as aesthetic reference and not as a
   technical limit.
3. **What is on disk.** Head and Heels as cartoon blobs with eyes, and 24 flat
   fills.

**Verdict: (2).** Decided by the operator.

## Why, and what it costs

It is the project's own written plan, so choosing it makes `REIMAGINING.md` the
specification rather than one document competing with two others.

It is also the cheaper direction technically, and for a non-obvious reason. The
64x32 tile was the constraint that made the AI pipeline hard. Models generate
high-resolution isometric assets far better than they generate 1987-constrained
sprite sheets. "2026" is the direction in which `ai_sprites.py` becomes viable
rather than the direction that fights it.

The cost is real and is accepted: the character art on disk is thrown away. That
art is genuinely drawn — it is not part of the 24 placeholders — and it is the only
drawn entity art in the project. Its style is unrelated to the reference in any of
the ten screenshots, so keeping it would mean keeping a fourth language.

## What was already true before this decision

The fidelity argument cuts both ways and was worth checking: you cannot lose
fidelity you never had. At the moment of the decision the screen showed coloured
rectangles for every entity and no walls at all. There was no 1987 look to
preserve.

So this is not a downgrade. It is the first position from which an upgrade is
possible.

## What this decision does not do

It does not make the art appear. The pipeline is candidate-only and **no art has
ever been accepted** — 65 sprite files, 24 of them flat fills listed in
`scripts/art_content_baseline.txt` as debt.

It also does not retcon the walls. `castle_start.tmx` has `Floor` and `Walls`
layers and `isBlocked` reads `Walls` for collision, so the walls decide where the
party cannot go and are never rendered. Whatever language is chosen, that is a
rendering bug and it is independent of the art pipeline. It is the cheapest
visible improvement available and it needs no new assets.

## What has teeth now, and what does not

- `scripts/validate_art_content.py`, in `make check`, rejects a flat fill that is
  not already listed as debt. It decides *this is not art*. It cannot decide
  whether art is good.
- `docs/art-contact-sheet.png` and `docs/ART_REVIEW.md` put every sprite in front of
  a person. Colour count cannot tell a good sprite from a bad one, and the sheet is
  the half that a human answers.

A candidate reaching the manifest without either of those having been satisfied is
the failure mode T098 documents, and it has happened once already.

## The constraint that survives this decision

The 1987 originals do not enter this project as art, as controls, as a LoRA, or as
an img2img target. Referencing them to know what to build is not using them. That
distinction held throughout the work that produced this decision — the screenshots
were read outside the repository and nothing was derived from them pixel-wise.
